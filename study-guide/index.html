New Jersey permit test study guide

The NJ knowledge test is broader than signs and common-sense road rules. This guide organizes the New Jersey-specific facts that are easiest to miss, then links each subject to focused practice.

Start with the test target

You need 40 correct answers out of 50.That is an 80% passing score, so you can miss no more than 10 scored questions.

A good readiness target is stronger than the minimum. Aim for at least 45/50 on several fresh mock exams, then review every miss. Repeating one memorized set can make your score look better without improving your coverage.

1. GDL permits and restrictions

New Jersey's Graduated Driver License rules generate many state-specific questions. Know who can supervise a permit holder, the restricted hours, passenger limits, decal rules, and how long supervised practice lasts.

  • A supervising driver must be at least 21, hold a valid New Jersey license, and have at least three years of driving experience.
  • Most GDL permit and probationary drivers under 21 may not drive from 11:01 p.m. to 5:00 a.m.
  • Drivers under 21 subject to GDL rules must display one red reflective decal on each license plate.
  • Under-21 permit holders need at least 50 hours of supervised practice, including 10 hours during darkness.

2. Signs, signals, and pavement markings

Study both recognition and action. Shape and color help you identify a sign before you can read it, but the exam can also describe a symbol in words.

  • An octagon is used only for STOP signs; a downward-pointing triangle means YIELD.
  • A yellow diamond warns of hazards. Orange diamonds identify construction or maintenance warnings.
  • A yellow pennant marks a no-passing zone. A round yellow sign warns of a railroad crossing.
  • Yellow center lines divide opposing traffic; white lane lines separate traffic moving in the same direction.
  • When one center line is broken and the other is solid, only traffic beside the broken line may pass when safe.

3. Right-of-way and intersections

Right-of-way is never permission to drive carelessly. At an uncontrolled intersection, reduce speed, look both ways, and prepare to stop. If two vehicles arrive together, the driver on the left generally yields to the driver on the right.

New Jersey law distinguishes crosswalk duties: motorists must stop and remain stopped for a pedestrian in a marked crosswalk, while motorists must yield the right-of-way to a pedestrian in an unmarked crosswalk at an intersection.

5. Alcohol, drugs, points, and penalties

These questions are especially easy to underestimate because many answers are New Jersey-specific. Know the BAC thresholds, implied consent, point values, surcharge triggers, probationary-driver consequences, and the fact that New Jersey does not issue conditional or work licenses to suspended drivers.

  • For drivers age 21 or older, a BAC of 0.08% or higher can result in a DUI conviction.
  • Drivers under 21 are subject to New Jersey's lower zero-tolerance threshold.
  • Refusing a breath test after a lawful DUI arrest carries separate consequences under implied consent.
  • Alcohol impairment begins before a driver necessarily feels drunk; coffee, cold showers, and fresh air do not remove alcohol.

6. Safe driving and emergencies

Learn the sequence of actions, not just one isolated step. For a skid, steer in the direction the rear of the vehicle is moving. For a tire blowout, hold the wheel firmly, keep straight, and slow gradually without braking hard. With ABS, apply steady brake pressure instead of pumping.

The current manual identifies four distraction types: visual, manual, cognitive, and auditory. Handheld-phone use is a primary offense, with exceptions limited to listed emergencies.

7. Trucks, buses, motorcycles, and vulnerable road users

If you cannot see a truck driver's face in a mirror, the driver likely cannot see you. Stay out of front, rear, and side no-zones; never enter the space to the right of a truck beginning a right turn.

New Jersey's Safe Passing Law requires moving over when possible or leaving at least four feet when passing a vulnerable road user. If four feet is not available, slow to 25 mph and be prepared to stop until the pass is safe.

A study plan that exposes weak spots

  1. Take a balanced Quick 10 without looking up answers.
  2. Open the category where you missed the most questions and study until the explanations make sense.
  3. Review the missed-question queue instead of immediately resetting your progress.
  4. Take a fresh 50-question mock exam. Repeat until you score comfortably above 40 several times.
  5. Read the official manual sections behind any answer you still find surprising.
